-- A. FIX C: THE NUMBER scripted_null/1 DISCARDED --

THE AS-RUN CODE.
    scripted_null(Starts) ->
        [{K, win_rate(heldout({script, K}, ?FLOOR, Starts))}
         || K <- robo_gauntlet:kinds(), K =/= ?FLOOR].

win_rate/1 is element(1, rates(Os)) and rates/1 returns {W, L, D}. So the
loss rate and the draw rate were computed and then thrown away on the same
line. The feed's N line (exp066_floor_feed.txt lines 16..19) carries four win
rates and nothing else.

WHY THAT IS NOT COSMETIC. W = 0.0 is consistent with 160 losses and with 160
draws. The first says the floor bot BEATS that rung, which is an EDGE. The
second says the pair is parked and there is NO edge. One leg of every
candidate intransitive triple that runs through the floor bot is exactly that
distinction, so the as-run record could not close such a triple even when the
other two legs were sitting in it.

-- D. THE RELATION, STATED ONCE, AND THE EDGES IT PRODUCES --

THE RELATION.

    A beats B  iff  W(A,B) > 0.5

where W(A,B) is A's win rate over the 160 matches of the ordered cell (A,B),
and a WIN requires B dead and A alive at match end. DRAWS COUNT AS NOT
BEATING. That is exp066's convention for its primary endpoint throughout: a
draw is not a win, so it cannot help A beat B any more than a loss can. A
turn cap with both tanks alive, and a mutual death, are both draws.

WHY THIS READING AND NOT A LOOSER ONE. Because W(A,B) > 0.5 implies
W(A,B) > L(A,B) while the reverse does not, this is the STRICTER of the two
readings available, so nothing found below is an artifact of a permissive
relation. At most one direction of a pair can satisfy it, since
W(A,B) + W(B,A) =< 1. A pair where neither direction satisfies it carries NO
EDGE, which is the honest reading of a pair parked in draws. NO SECOND
RELATION IS DEFINED ANYWHERE IN THIS RECORD.

-- F. FIX D: THE MATCH-LEVEL NULL WAS MIS-SCALED BY TWO --

WHAT WAS WRONG. xp_from_pairs/2 built a synthetic tournament by storing a
MARGIN V at cell {I,J} and -V at cell {J,I}. xp_mg/1 is a WIN-RATE
differencing operator, wr(M,I,J) - wr(M,J,I), so applied to that matrix it
returned V - (-V) = 2V. Every synthetic margin was DOUBLE the margin it
represented, so every band was effectively HALVED against the synthetic
nulls: the record's band-0.10 match-level row is really a band-0.05 row. The
OBSERVED matrix holds win rates (xp_at/3 puts W(I,J) in the upper half and
L(I,J) in the lower half) and is differenced correctly, so the observed
counts are NOT affected.

THE FIX IS AT THE SOURCE AND IT IS THE UNITS. xp_from_wins/2 stores the two
WIN RATES of a synthetic cell, W(I,J) and 1.0 - W(I,J), which is exactly what
xp_matrix/2 stores for the observed matrix. One differencing operator is then
correct for both and a band means the same thing on both. There is NO
compensating factor anywhere: xp_null_match/3 now draws a win rate,
xp_flips(M) / M, and xp_null_sign/2 draws xp_coin_w/0, which is 0.0 or 1.0.
Antisymmetry is still by construction, the two cells of a pair summing to 1.0.

HOW THE TWO COLUMNS BELOW ARE OBTAINED. The as-run construction is kept
verbatim as xp_from_pairs_as_run/2 and is called only here. Each column is
drawn from a FRESHLY seeded generator, seed 660, 200 draws, 20 champions, 160
matches per cell, and the two constructions consume rand identically (one
rand:uniform(2) per coin, 160 per cell), so a given null's two columns are the
SAME draws under two encodings.

THE DOUBLING, SHOWN RATHER THAN ARGUED. The span of |margin| over every pair
of every draw:

F1b. THE CORRECTED COLUMN DOES NOT REPRODUCE THE INDEPENDENT SCRIPT AT EVERY
BAND, AND THE REASON IS NOT THE FIX.

scripts/exp066_verify_null_scaling.escript builds its corrected column a THIRD
way: it HALVES the margin and stores plus and minus half, so xp_mg/1 returns
the margin once. That is algebraically the same margin as xp_from_wins/2's, and
at band 0.05 the two agree digit for digit. At bands 0.10 and 0.15 they do NOT.
The two encodings agree on all three bands: false.

WHY. Every synthetic margin is (2K - M)/M for an integer K, and all three
pre-registered bands are exact multiples of the margin quantum 2/160: 0.05 is
8/160, 0.10 is 16/160, 0.15 is 24/160. So every draw contains edges whose
margin equals the band EXACTLY, where 'margin > band' is mathematically FALSE.
None of 0.05, 0.10 and 0.15 is a binary float, so each encoding renders those
boundary edges slightly above or slightly below the band, and the two round in
OPPOSITE directions on the negative side. Two worked examples, both exact
ties in arithmetic:
  K = 72 of 160, true margin -0.1 exactly. Halved encoding gives
    -0.09999999999999998, win-rate encoding -0.10000000000000003. The reverse
    edge is therefore NOT decisive at band 0.10 under the first and IS under
    the second.
  K = 68 of 160, true margin -0.15 exactly. Halved gives
    -0.15000000000000002, win-rate -0.14999999999999997, and the reverse edge
    flips the other way at band 0.15.
  K = 84 of 160, true margin +0.05 exactly. BOTH encodings give
    0.050000000000000044, so both count an exact tie as decisive at band 0.05.
    Neither encoding is right there; they merely agree.

NEITHER FLOAT ENCODING IS CORRECT ON EVERY BOUNDARY EDGE, and which ties each
one counts varies by band. scripts/exp066_verify_recovered.escript check 5
enumerates them: at band 0.05 both encodings count both ties (K = 76 and 84)
as decisive and both are wrong; at 0.10 the win-rate form counts both ties
(72 and 88) and the halved form one (88), so both are wrong and the halved one
less so; at 0.15 the win-rate form counts NEITHER tie and is right while the
halved form counts one (68) and is wrong. This is a property of the
PRE-REGISTERED COUNTER, which tests a strict float inequality against a band
that is not a binary float, and NOT of this fix. The counter is NOT touched
